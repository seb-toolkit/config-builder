What Safe Exam Browser can and cannot protect

Published 2026-11-26 · Unofficial guide

Staff are often asked “does SEB stop cheating?”. The honest answer depends on the threat. This guide goes through the common ones, grouped by how much SEB helps. For the wider picture, including the research, see secure exam environments.

Settings matter. What SEB does depends on how the .seb file is configured and on the operating system. Test your own file (see how to do a test run) rather than assuming.

What SEB is good at

ThreatWhat SEB does
Student opens another program on the exam computer (notes, chat, a calculator you have not allowed)Runs full screen and, with the usual settings, stops other programs being started or used. You can allow named programs.
Student opens other websites in the same browserThe URL filter blocks pages that are not on your allow list. See the URL filter guide.
Copy and paste to or from other applicationsCan be limited to inside the exam, or blocked.
Screenshots, screen sharing and screen recording of the examSettings exist to block them (for example allowScreenCapture and allowScreenSharing). Check they behave as you expect on each operating system.
Running the exam inside a virtual machineSettings exist to refuse to start in one (allowVirtualMachine). A determined student may still get round this, so treat it as a hurdle.
Printing, saving and sharing exam pagesCan be switched off.
Students wandering off through the Moodle menusBlock the Moodle areas you do not want. See keeping students inside the quiz.

What SEB makes harder, but does not stop

ThreatWhy it is only partly covered
Altered or old copies of SEBMoodle can check the keys that SEB sends, and you can set a minimum SEB version. But the keys are calculated from the settings file, so someone who has the file can in principle work them out. The keys raise the bar for casual attempts. They are not a strong guarantee. See the research summary.
Students finding a way into blocked sitesA URL filter blocks by address. New AI sites, mirrors and proxies appear all the time, which is why an allow list is better than a block list.
Sharing the quit passwordA password given to the room can be passed on. Hand it out only as students finish, or use the quit-link route.
Students on their own computersYou control what SEB does, not what is installed underneath. A managed lab computer is much easier to trust than a personal laptop.

What SEB cannot do

  • See or control a second device. A phone, tablet or other laptop beside the exam computer is out of reach, including for AI assistants. This is the biggest gap.
  • Stop help from another person in the room, on a call or through a message.
  • Know who is sitting the exam. It does not check identity. Moodle sign-in, invigilators or proctoring must do that.
  • Detect cheating by itself. By default it does not record what a student types or sees. Logging student activity needs SEB Server and has privacy consequences.
  • Fix a badly designed exam. Questions that can be answered by lookup are the same questions under lockdown. Assessment design does more than any setting.
  • Protect the exam outside SEB. If the quiz can also be opened in a normal browser, it is not protected. Moodle’s SEB requirement is what enforces this. A hidden quiz alone does not.

A sensible way to use it

  1. Use SEB for what it does well: close off the exam computer and the open web.
  2. Add the layers it cannot give you: invigilation or proctoring, randomised questions, time limits and questions that ask for reasoning.
  3. Tell students what is and is not allowed, and give them a way to ask for help. See the student guides.
  4. Run a test run on every type of computer before the real exam.
