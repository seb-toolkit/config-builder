Signing in to Microsoft inside Safe Exam Browser: what students need to know

Published 2026-11-26 · Unofficial guide for students

Many universities use a Microsoft account to sign in to Moodle and other exam systems. Signing in inside Safe Exam Browser (SEB) can behave differently from signing in with your usual browser, and it is a common place for problems to appear. A little preparation avoids nearly all of them.

Your university’s instructions come first. The sign-in methods allowed in SEB are chosen by whoever set up your exam.

Why it can be different

SEB blocks other programs, and some sign-in methods rely on separate programs or system windows. For example, signing in with a fingerprint, face recognition, a PIN or a passkey makes Windows open its own sign-in window, which SEB can interfere with unless the exam has been set up to allow it. Approving a sign-in on your phone works the same way in SEB as anywhere else, because the phone does the work.

How you sign inIn SEB
Password plus an approval on your phone (authenticator app)Usually the most reliable. Have your phone charged and with you.
Windows Hello (face, fingerprint, PIN) or a passkey on WindowsWorks only if the exam allows it. Test it first.
A passkey or security key on a Mac or iPadMay not work in SEB. Use phone approval instead if you can.
A code texted to your phoneNormally fine, if your phone has a signal.

What to do before the exam

  1. Do the practice quiz in SEB, signing in the same way you will on the day. If you cannot sign in, tell the exam office then, while it can still be fixed.
  2. Know your method. Check in your account’s security settings which sign-in methods you have set up. Having a phone approval or authenticator app as a backup is a good idea.
  3. Keep your phone ready. Charged, with a signal, and with the authenticator app working. Check your exam rules about phones: some allow a phone for sign-in only, others want it away from you after you sign in.
  4. Sign in well before the start time. If your session is already open when the exam starts, you may not be asked again. Do not rely on that.

If sign-in fails in SEB

  • Read the message, and try again slowly. A mistyped password or a prompt you missed on your phone is the usual reason.
  • If a window you expected does not appear (for example a Windows Hello or passkey prompt), choose the option to sign in another way and use your phone or a code.
  • If you are locked out or cannot get any option to work, tell the invigilator or help desk straight away. Say which method you tried and what appeared.
  • Never ask someone else to sign in for you, and do not share your password or codes. That counts as breaking the exam rules.

A quick note on privacy

SEB works with the sign-in pages your exam site gives it. It does not add its own sign-in, and your password is typed on the same Microsoft page as usual. If you have doubts about what is recorded during your exam, ask your university: it is their decision and their policy.
