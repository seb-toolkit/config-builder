Before your Safe Exam Browser exam: a student checklist

Published 2026-11-26 · Unofficial guide for students

Safe Exam Browser (SEB) is a locked-down web browser. It fills your screen, closes off other programs and websites, and opens only your exam. Most problems on the day come from things that could have been sorted out a few days earlier. Work through this list in good time.

Your university’s instructions come first. Every exam is set up differently. If this page disagrees with what your lecturer or exam office told you, follow them.

A week before

  • Find your instructions. They are usually on the course page or in the exam notice: which version of SEB to install, which computer types are allowed, and who to contact.
  • Install SEB from the official site. Download it from safeexambrowser.org or from the link your university gives you. Do not use download sites that bundle other software.
  • Use a computer you can rely on. A laptop that is fully updated, has enough free disk space and can run for the whole exam. If your university supplies computers, find out where and when you can try one.
  • Do the practice quiz. If there is one, sit it in SEB, start to finish. This is the single most useful thing you can do. It shows up installation, sign-in and network problems while there is still time to fix them.

The day before

  • Update, then leave it alone. Install pending system updates now, not an hour before the exam.
  • Check your sign-in. Make sure you can sign in to your university account and that you know where your approval prompts go. See signing in to Microsoft inside SEB.
  • Check your internet. Use a connection you trust. A cable or a strong, close Wi-Fi signal is better than a shared or busy one.
  • Charge and plug in. Bring the charger. Laptops that run out of battery mid-exam are the commonest avoidable problem.
  • Write down who to contact and how, in case you cannot get into the exam: the exam office, the lecturer or the IT help desk.

Just before the exam

  • Close everything else. SEB may refuse to start, or may ask you to close programs, if screen sharing, remote-access tools, virtual machines or other restricted programs are running. Save your work and close them.
  • Turn off what could interrupt you. Notifications, chat apps, automatic updates and screen recorders.
  • Start from the right place. Most Moodle exams have a Launch Safe Exam Browser button on the quiz page. If you open the quiz in an ordinary browser, Moodle tells you to use SEB.
  • Allow time. Open SEB at least 15 minutes before the start so a slow start does not use up exam time.

During the exam

  • You cannot use other programs or websites unless your instructions say you can. That is normal, not a fault.
  • Do not try to get around SEB. Attempts to get round it can be flagged, and a problem caused by trying is much harder to put right than a plain technical fault.
  • Stay calm if something goes wrong. Do not close SEB, restart or switch off unless you are told to. Put your hand up or contact the invigilator or exam office. Moodle usually saves your answers as you go, and your invigilator can tell you what happens to the clock.

When you finish

  • Submit your attempt in the quiz as the instructions say, and check the confirmation.
  • To leave SEB, use the Exit Safe Exam Browser button if the quiz page shows one, or the quit button in SEB. You may need a quit password, which the invigilator gives you. See if SEB won’t start or won’t quit.

If you cannot use SEB

Tell your university well before the exam. If you have an accessibility need, a computer that cannot run SEB or any other reason, there is usually an alternative arrangement, but only if they know in time.
